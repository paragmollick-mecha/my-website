A Bit About Me

Who am I?

Parag Mollick is a senior Mechanical Engineering undergraduate at BUET, Dhaka. His academic focus centers on thermo-fluid system modeling, CFD-driven heat transfer analysis, and thermal management for data centers. Along with basic ANSYS proficiency, he is a Certified SOLIDWORKS Associate (CSWA), and has recently co-developed an electromechanical, drone-based tranquilization system featuring a custom coil gun for remote wildlife management. He possesses strong CAD modeling expertise. Most recently, he led the design and validation of a Rear Door Heat Exchanger (RDHx) for data centre server cooling — a cost-effective, automotive-radiator-inspired alternative to direct-to-chip liquid cooling, capable of neutralizing up to 5 kW of server heat load. The project combined analytical heat exchanger sizing, 3D CFD simulation in ANSYS Fluent, 1D transient system modeling in GT-SUITE, and hands-on fabrication and hydrostatic testing of a working physical prototype. He has also worked on the structural and fatigue analysis of a spiral-jaw mechanical clutch, where a three-tier validation methodology — theoretical hand calculations, individual-component FEA, and full-assembly simulation in ANSYS — was used to verify the static strength and fatigue life of all critical components under cyclic torsional loading.

What I've Built

Projects

AeroMark ME 366 Project

AeroMark is an FPV drone-based wildlife management system designed to remotely tranquilize and safely immobilize wild animals for research and conservation, rescue, and veterinary support. The drone carries a lightweight electromagnetic coil gun and a stabilized camera for accurate targeting. The coil gun works by boosting a 12V supply to high voltage, charging capacitors, and discharging them through a solenoid to generate a magnetic field that launches a metal dart. The system reduces direct human-animal interaction, improves researcher safety, and enables efficient field operations such as animal tagging, monitoring, and health assessment. The drone has a flight time of approximately 10 minutes, a range of 1 km, and manual control with an FPV camera system. Future developments include AI-assisted targeting, sensor upgrades, automatic dosing, swarm operations, and advanced data logging. Current limitations include limited range and accuracy, dependence on weather conditions, the need for skilled operators, and potential GPS signal issues in dense environments.
